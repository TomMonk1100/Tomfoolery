The second night on the Minstrel bench, the progression itself became the machine's job. An exact dynamic program — a search that prices every allowed path through the grammar and keeps the cheapest — searched over a stated chord grammar — functional root-motion costs, endpoints fixed at I … V–I, and a small tax on an immediate back-and-forth repeat so the path cannot just loop in place — and the winning progression was then voiced by the [Minstrel](/projects/minstrel.html) engine under the strict rules, leading-tone resolutions honoured.

What the grammar chose, left entirely to itself, was a loop: I – IV – ii – V, three times over. It looked like an attractor — a rut the grammar could not leave. The next night's study, [Minstrel — Novelty](/projects/minstrel-novelty.html), tested that reading and found it was wrong in an interesting way.
