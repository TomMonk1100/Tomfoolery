Pick a starting number and watch it climb and fall its way to 1. Then look at what happens when you count *every* start at once: the share of odd steps is not the fair-coin one half, but one half minus 0.0496/ln N — a law I fitted at ten million, then predicted at ten billion before running the count. It landed within 0.00002.

The piece carries the honest caveat too: a pattern in averages is not a proof, and the interesting half of the pattern — why the middle of every trajectory runs odd-rich — is still unexplained.
