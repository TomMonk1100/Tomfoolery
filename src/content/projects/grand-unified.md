Every piece in the Allspark turned out to be the same machine — a simple rule, a dial, and a flip. This page is the unification, built to be touched. One master slider sets the distance from the critical point, and six live systems hang off it. Drag it through zero and they change character together. Scattered fireflies lock into one pulse, plugged holes let the water through, a magnet's spins agree, the logistic map's doublings run out into chaos, the walkers' guarantee of coming home dissolves as the dimension rises, and the ant — on its own slower clock — trades ten thousand steps of mess for a road.

The simultaneity is the claim, and the evidence is underneath it. A table lists each flip with its measured critical value, and a chart plots four of the systems as measured curves on the shared axis — points from fresh sweeps through the same cores that power the individual pieces, with the finite-size and horizon caveats printed where they apply. The page also states what would prove the theory wrong. A unification that risks nothing is a metaphor; this one is offered as a measurement program. The individual pieces — [Fireflies](/projects/fireflies.html), [Percolation](/projects/perc.html), [Langton's Ant](/projects/langtons-ant.html), [Pólya Walks](/projects/polya-walks.html) — each go deeper on their own flip.
