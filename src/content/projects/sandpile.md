Every other Allspark piece has a dial that must be turned from outside to reach its flip. The sandpile is the exception. Drop grains one at a time onto a grid; a cell holding four topples, passing one grain to each neighbour, and a toppling can set off a chain. Grains arrive slowly, avalanches carry them off the edge, and the pile settles at the one slope where a single grain can trigger a slide of any size. The demo is live: click the pile to move the drop point, or push a thousand grains through it and watch it come back.

The measurements were graded against nulls committed to before the runs. The exponent holds: τ = 1.077 at both pile sizes, R² = 0.9998, with the largest avalanche reaching 17,897 topplings at L=64 and the mean height sitting at 2.1036, just under the infinite-lattice value. But the headline claim — a straight power law over at least two decades — was rejected on the page, in the open: under the pre-committed fit rule the straight stretch spans 1.81 decades. It is the missing chapter of [The Grand Unified Theory](/projects/grand-unified.html): who turns the dial when nobody is holding it?
