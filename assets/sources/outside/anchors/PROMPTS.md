# Breckenridge sky anchor prompts

Generated with the built-in image generation workflow. All four raster plates
are 1983 × 793 PNG files.

These are art-direction anchors. Before using them for live crossfades, the
landscape should be registered to one canonical foreground plate and the final
delivery assets should be color-matched and compressed.

## Canonical master — clear noon

Create a premium editorial landscape photograph of Breckenridge, Texas at clear
midday, intended as the locked master composition for a living weather almanac.
Show authentic western Cross Timbers ranchland: gently rolling dry-gold summer
pasture, scattered mature post oaks and mesquite, a subtle distant suggestion of
Hubbard Creek water, a low natural ridgeline, and an immense Texas sky occupying
roughly seventy percent of the frame. Use very wide panoramic hero framing from
a fixed eye-level camera, with the horizon across the lower third, layered depth,
calm left-center copy space, and balanced interest toward the right. The light
should feel clear and hot but natural, with the sun outside the frame. Use a
restrained premium editorial grade. No text, people, structures, roads, visible
sun, moon, logos, or watermark. Avoid Colorado mountains, desert clichés,
exaggerated HDR, fantasy, illustration, or game-engine rendering.

deep cobalt upper sky, restrained lavender transition, faint warm peach at the
eastern horizon, and readable cool shadow detail across the pasture. Preserve
the camera, crop, horizon, lake, hills, tree locations, foreground geometry,
proportions, and copy space. No visible sun, moon, stars, artificial lights,
text, structures, or people. Avoid neon purple, crushed shadows, heavy fog,
day-for-night filtering, fantasy, or geometry changes.

## Golden hour with scattered clouds

Using the clear-noon master as the edit target, change only illumination and sky
weather. Create late-afternoon golden hour with low warm light from outside the
right edge, long delicate shadows, amber rim light on grasses and oak canopies,
and realistic atmospheric depth. Add a limited field of naturally scaled
scattered cumulus and thin high cloud with luminous edges and cool dimensional
undersides while keeping broad open sky and left-center copy space. Preserve all
landscape geometry and framing. No visible sun, moon, stars, precipitation,
text, structures, or people. Avoid a global orange filter, oversaturation,
fantasy clouds, giant cloud ceilings, exaggerated HDR, or geometry changes.

## Approaching storm
