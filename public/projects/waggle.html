Explain it like I'm 5

A bee finds good flowers and flies home. In the dark hive she dances a little figure eight on the wall. The straight middle bit of the dance points the way: tilted from straight-up by exactly the angle the flowers sit from the sun. And the longer she wiggles through that middle bit, the farther away the flowers are. Her sisters watch, then fly out and find the flowers. It is a map, danced.

Why you're looking at this

I like codes that fit in two numbers. This one is an angle and a duration, swapped from sunlight into gravity in the dark, and it held up when people finally tested it properly — including the strange part, which is that the bee's distance is not really distance. Her odometer counts how much scenery streamed past her eyes, so a short flight through a cluttered tunnel gets danced as a long one. The page runs the code both ways, with the calibration printed and its wobble admitted, because the wobble is part of the finding.

the calibration this page uses

Distance is read from waggle-run duration with one straight line, the same line in both directions:

waggle duration = 250 ms + 0.75 ms × distance in metres

The slope is von Frisch's figure for his bees (Apis mellifera carnica): roughly 75 ms of extra waggle per 100 m of flight. The 250 ms intercept is a modelling choice, not a quoted constant — real calibration curves do not start at zero, and 250 ms makes the line pass through Seeley's rule of thumb, about 1 second of waggle for 1 km (Seeley, The Wisdom of the Hive, 1995). Food closer than 50 m gets the round dance instead — von Frisch's switch distance — which says "near, go search by smell" and carries no direction.

the catch: the odometer reads light, not ground

Von Frisch first thought distance was measured by energy spent. It is not. Srinivasan, Zhang, Altwein and Tautz (2000, Science 287: 851–853) flew bees through a narrow tunnel — 6.4 m long, 11 cm wide — lined with a busy pattern. The scenery streaming past at close range piled up so much optic flow that the bees came home and danced for a flight of roughly 186–195 m, after actually flying 6 m. Lined with stripes running along the flight path, so almost nothing streamed sideways, the same bees mostly did the round dance: the odometer had barely registered. Their calibration, in the units the bee actually uses: 1 ms of waggle ≈ 17.7° of image motion across the eye.

So what the dance encodes is experienced optic flow, which only matches ground distance when the flight is ordinary outdoor flight. The same distance over water or from high up dances shorter; through clutter it dances longer. The encode/decode above is the code for ordinary flight — the tunnel is the proof of what the code is really counting.

notes

  • There is no universal calibration. The 0.75 ms/m slope is von Frisch's, for one subspecies. Other races run measurably different slopes — "dance dialects" — and Srinivasan's own outdoor bees gave 1.88 ms/m in low, cluttered flight. A modern single-colony curve (Carniolan bees, Kohl & Rutschmann 2021) runs 0.41 s at 100 m to 2.20 s at 1.7 km, steeper than the line used here. Treat every distance on this page as "on von Frisch's calibration", not as bee truth.
  • The round/waggle switch is also approximate: von Frisch put it at ~50 m; other accounts say 50–75 m or under 80 m, and some describe a transitional sickle dance in between, which this page does not model. Modern work argues the round dance is not a wholly separate, information-free dance either. The 50 m line here is von Frisch's figure, chosen and labelled.
  • Nothing here claims bees "understand geometry". The mechanism is a transposition — a sun-compass bearing re-expressed against gravity, an optic-flow total re-expressed as a run duration — carried out by sensory and motor machinery. The page describes the mechanism, not an inner mathematician.
  • Real dances are noisy: individual runs scatter around the mean angle and duration, and recruits finish the job with odour and landmarks. This page decodes the idealised code exactly (the core's round-trip is exact by construction and gate-tested); a real follower bee gets an approximation.
  • Dance vigour also varies with food quality (Seeley 1995) — a real part of the signal, deliberately out of scope here. The controls carry angle and duration only.
