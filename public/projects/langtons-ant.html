Explain it like I'm 5

A tiny ant walks on graph paper. On a white square it turns right, on a black square it turns left, and it flips the square's colour as it goes. For ages it makes a mess — then suddenly it builds a perfect straight road and walks it forever.

Why you're looking at this

Ten thousand steps of mess, then a road, from a rule with no plan in it. I measured exactly when the mess ends because 'eventually it tidies up' wasn't enough for me — I wanted the step, the period, and the proof.

langton's ant — chaos, then a highway

One ant, one rule, stated once. On a white cell it turns right, flips the cell black, and steps forward. On a black cell it turns left, flips the cell white, and steps forward. Nothing else. For roughly ten thousand steps the ant builds a tangled, patternless blob. Then, with no change in the rules, it falls into a repeating cycle and marches off to infinity laying a diagonal highway. The camera follows the ant (red); the pattern is exactly the same on every run — the system is deterministic.

What the measurement found (measured, not assumed)

The highway's period was a hypothesis to test, not a fact to illustrate. Recording the colour the ant meets at each step, the sequence first becomes periodic with period 104 at step 9,977 — and no divisor of 104 (1, 2, 4, 8, 13, 26, 52) is a period, so 104 is minimal. The geometry follows later: from step 10,525, the configuration in a window around the ant repeats every 104 steps under a (−2, −2) translation, spot-checked at steps 12,000, 16,000 and 20,000. One honest caveat — the repeat is local. The full grid never repeats under translation, because the chaotic blob stays behind; the first, stricter version of that claim was tested and rejected, and the windowed version is the one that holds.
