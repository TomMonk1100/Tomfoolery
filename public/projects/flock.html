Explain it like I'm 5

Imagine a crowd of birds where every bird does just one thing — it turns to fly the same way as the birds right next to it. But the world is noisy, and the noise keeps shoving their headings around. Quiet world: the whole crowd becomes one flock, flowing one way. Noisy world: everybody mills about. And there is an exact amount of noise where it flips from one to the other.

Why you're looking at this

The fireflies synchronised in time — scattered blinking becoming one pulse. I wanted to know whether the same trick works in direction: whether "everyone face the same way" is also an order that switches on at a sharp point. It is. This is the piece where the flock decides — and near the deciding point, you can watch it change its mind over and over.

the flock flip

2026-11-16 · Vicsek-style flocking · the dial is noise · every number below is measured

Three hundred agents fly at constant speed in a periodic box. Each one steers toward the average heading of its neighbours within radius 1 — itself included — and then the world shoves its heading by a random amount drawn from a window of width η, the noise. That is the entire rule set. The order parameter φ is the mean resultant length of the heading vectors: 0 when headings cancel out, 1 when all 300 point the same way. Colour on the canvas is heading, so order is something you can see — a milling rainbow, or one colour flowing.

η = 5.20

The swarm starts in disorder. Drag the noise down through ηc and a single flock assembles itself out of the milling — nobody leads it. Drag back up and it melts. Near the critical value, expect the flock to take its time deciding, and to change its mind: that indecision is measured, not staged.

What the sweep found (measured, not tuned)

The sweep ran the same core that animates the canvas: N = 300 agents in a 20 × 20 periodic box (density 0.75, radius 1, speed 0.1), three seeds per point, 800 steps discarded as transient and φ averaged over the next 800. The order falls from φ = 0.998 at η = 0 to φ = 0.052 at η = 6.0, and crosses φ = 0.5 at ηc ≈ 1.648 (interpolated between the measured points at 1.60 and 1.65).

The honest shape: at this size the flip is steep but smeared, not a cliff. The seed-mean curve descends over a broad shoulder, and between η ≈ 1.65 and 2.15 it wiggles instead of marching — 0.497, 0.515, 0.516, 0.490, 0.443 — with the largest seed-to-seed spread of the whole sweep (sd up to 0.074). A single long run parked at η = 1.65 shows why: φ itself wanders between 0.073 and 0.783 (mean 0.487, sd 0.142) over 2,000 steps. Near the flip, the flock cannot settle — the fluctuations grow until they are nearly the size of the signal. A hysteresis probe found the two starts agree at η = 1.6 and 1.8, but at η = 2.0 a flock that starts aligned keeps φ = 0.383 while one that starts scattered only reaches 0.285 in the same window — a formed flock is slightly harder to melt than an unformed one is to assemble, at least on this timescale. Whether the underlying transition is truly continuous or weakly jumpy cannot be settled at N = 300; the literature's larger systems form travelling bands and sharpen. What is measured here is a finite flock, and it is reported as one.

The same machine

This is the grand unified theory with feathers. A simple rule — copy your neighbours. A dial — the noise η. A flip — at ηc ≈ 1.648, shared direction switches on. The fireflies' order was a moment in time, everyone flashing together; the magnet's was a direction in spin space; this one's is a direction in plain space, the first order in the Allspark you could point at. The dial here runs backwards compared to the fireflies — turn it down and order appears — but a dial does not care which end is which. What matters is that there is a value where the system's whole character turns over, and this page measured it instead of assuming it.

The theory on the grand unified page names its own fingerprints: near a real critical point, fluctuations should grow and the system should slow down. The flock shows both, uninvited. Park the dial at ηc and φ wanders across almost its whole range, and a melted flock takes visibly longer to re-form there than it does deep in the quiet. A metaphor would not bother misbehaving in exactly the predicted way.

Methods and caveats

  • ηc is defined as the φ = 0.5 crossing of the seed-mean curve. It is a property of this flock — N = 300, density 0.75, radius 1, speed 0.1 — not a universal constant; change the density and it moves.
  • Finite size is the standing caveat: 300 agents round every sharp edge. The crossing window's wiggles (the seed-mean curve is not even monotone there) are printed in the table rather than smoothed away.
  • The sweep grid runs η = 0 to 6.0; the dial runs to 2π ≈ 6.28, where the noise window covers the full circle and headings are effectively randomised each step — the curve is already flat at its floor well before that.
  • Noise is uniform on [−η/2, +η/2], added after the neighbour average, and positions update with the new heading — the standard Vicsek step. The box is periodic; the flock that leaves one edge returns at the other.
