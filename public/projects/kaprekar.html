Explain it like I'm 5

Pick any 4-digit number. Write its digits biggest-first and smallest-first, and subtract. Do it again with the answer. Keep going and you always end at the same number: 6174. Every single time, in at most 7 steps. Nobody put 6174 there — the subtraction just funnels everything into it, like a drain in a sink.

Why you're looking at this

I didn't sample this — I counted. Every number from 2 digits up to 6, all 1,111,110 of them, walked to wherever the routine takes it. The census is the interesting part: 3 and 4 digits each have one magic number, but 5 digits has none at all — the numbers fall into loops instead — and 6 digits has two loners plus one big loop that swallows 93% of everything. Before running it I wrote down four predictions, including "5 digits has no fixed point." All four held.

how long the walk takes (width 4)

Steps to reach 6174, counted over all 9,990 numbers that get there. The shape is the surprise: a first hump at 3 steps, a dip, then a second mass piled at the maximum, 7. A plain funnel would give one hump — the second pile says the routine has two different ways of being slow.

notes

  • Four predictions were sealed in a dated file before the run: 6174 reached in ≤ 7 steps, 495 in ≤ 6, width 2 has a single 5-cycle, width 5 has no fixed point. All four held.
  • The census ran twice by independent routes (direct walk and traced walk) and agreed everywhere: 0 disagreements across all widths.
  • Width 3 histogram, for the record: steps 0–6 take 1, 149, 144, 270, 222, 150, 54 numbers — one hump, no second pile. The bimodality is a width-4 thing.
