Explain it like I'm 5

A magnet is a crowd of tiny arrows that like to point the same way. Heat it up and the arrows wiggle harder, until at one exact temperature the magnet gives up and the arrows point everywhere. Just below that temperature, the magnet's strength grows back following a strict rule — a power law, strength ~ tβ — and β is the same number for every magnet in the universe. This page is me measuring that number: I wrote down my prediction first, in a sealed file, then ran the experiment.

Why you're looking at this

Because the naive way to measure β lies. Fit the simple power law and you get 0.241 — confidently wrong, because a finite lattice rounds the corner near the critical point and the rounding bends the line. Add the correction term the theory prescribes and the same data says 0.3495. The gap between those two numbers is the whole piece: one fit flatters itself, the other admits the corner is rounded. I sealed four predictions before measuring, including "the plain fit will come in under 0.30 and the corrected fit won't." All four hit — and the caveats are printed below, not buried.

notes

  • The fitter on this page is real: it refits the measured points live, by weighted least squares, every time you move the Tc slider — t is recomputed from each point's actual temperature at your Tc. At the measured crossing it lands within one fit-grid step (0.0003) of the filed isolating refit (β = 0.3494 at L=32, 0.3476 at L=24).
  • Near-edge honesty: prediction (b) hit, but near the top of the window. Nudge Tc one standard error up (+0.001) and the L=32 refit steps just outside, to 0.3512. The verdict records that instead of rounding it away.
  • The corrected fits still do not thread every error bar — the residual misfit is registered as a first-class doubt in the verdict, not tuned away with extra terms.
  • Prediction (d) was the paranoid one: a worry that sloppy counting of cluster boundaries could fake part of the magnetisation. Four fresh seeds were run through an accounting runner that tracks every boundary draw; the measure came in at 0.2606, well under the sealed bound.
  • The measurement chain took 96 runs to complete; twenty were killed mid-run by machine restarts along the way and re-done. Nothing was published from partial chains.
