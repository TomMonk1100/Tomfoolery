Explain it like I'm 5

A magnet is made of tiny arrows that like to point the same way as their neighbours. Warm it up and the arrows start to wiggle. Right at one special temperature the magnet gives up — and just before it does, it cannot make up its mind. It flickers: almost all up, then patches of down, then almost settled again. The flickering is biggest exactly at that edge temperature, and the flicker is not just something you can see — you can measure it, and the measurement peaks.

Why you're looking at this

This page is an exhibit. Elsewhere on this site I keep a "grand unified theory" of small systems, and its most exposed claim is a falsifiable one: measurable flicker — heat capacity and susceptibility — must peak at the transition, at the temperature theory names in advance. This is the fingerprint exhibit for that claim. The chart below is not a sketch of what theory says should happen; it is what my simulation actually measured, seed by seed, spread bars and all. Drag the temperature slider through 2.269 and watch the live meters swell. If the peaks were not there, the claim would be dead. They are there.

Read it plainly: the heat capacity C(T) peaks exactly at the Tc grid point — 1.7028 at T = 2.269, up from 0.7572 at 2.0 and back down to 1.3733 at 2.4. The susceptibility χ(T) climbs through Tc (0.3707 at 2.0, 3.0604 at 2.2, 6.9225 at 2.269) and peaks one grid point above it, at 8.4212 at T = 2.4, before falling to 2.8630 at 2.7. That one-step offset is the finite lattice talking, and it is printed, not hidden.

notes

  • Caveat, printed as promised: a 20×20 lattice rounds and shifts the peaks. In the infinite lattice C diverges at Tc and χ peaks at Tc; here C peaks at the Tc grid point and the χ peak lands at the grid point just above Tc (2.4). Finite size is the whole reason — the correlation length cannot outgrow the box.
  • Caveat two: the spreads across only 3 seeds are wide near Tc — the χ range at 2.269 spans 5.31 to 8.35 (full spread 3.03), and at 2.2 it spans 2.24 to 4.65. Three seeds locate the peaks; they do not pin their heights. Do not quote the peak values to more precision than the bars allow.
  • The live panel runs the same Metropolis core as the sweep (the tested node core, 10 checks passing, ported inline — same seed gives the same run). Its meters use a rolling window of recent samples, so they lag a temperature change and wobble; that wobble is the phenomenon, not a bug.
  • The χ formula uses ⟨m²⟩ − ⟨|m|⟩², not ⟨m⟩²: below Tc the magnetisation flips sign between the two ordered states, and the naive formula would measure the ordered moment itself instead of the flicker around it. The core's harness pins both formulas against known cold and hot limits.
  • Equilibration matters: the sweep starts all-spins-up and discards 5,000 sweeps before sampling, because below Tc the ordered start measures equilibrium, while a random start can freeze into striped states that are not.
