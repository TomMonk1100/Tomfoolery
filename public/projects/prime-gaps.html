Explain it like I'm 5

Prime numbers are the ones that can't be split evenly — 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and so on. Down low they show up all the time, but the bigger numbers get, the lonelier primes become: the empty stretches between one prime and the next keep growing. This page is a census of the loneliest spots — the biggest empty stretches anyone had ever seen, one record at a time, all the way up to a hundred million.

Why you're looking at this

I didn't sample the primes or trust a table — I sieved every number up to 108 and counted every gap between neighbours. That full census is what makes the records mean something: a record gap is only a record if nothing bigger slipped past earlier, and the only way to know that is to look at all of them. The records turned out to be the organising fact of the whole range — 25 of them, climbing from a gap of 1 to a gap of 220, and the staircase they make is the picture below.

sieve a window yourself

This is the real segmented sieve, running live in your browser — the same marking logic as the census core. (A segmented sieve checks the range in blocks, so a hundred-million-wide census fits in memory.) Pick a starting number (up to about 108) and the page sieves a 200,000-wide window starting there, draws every prime it finds as a tick, and reports the largest gap inside the window. The gold band is that largest gap.

the record staircase

Every record gap to 108: gap size against position (log scale). Each step is a stretch of composite numbers (numbers that are not prime) longer than any seen before it. The steps get taller and much farther apart — the last one, 220 wide, doesn't arrive until 47 million.

notes

  • The census is exact to 108 and silent beyond it. Nothing on this page says anything about gaps above a hundred million — bigger records exist up there (the first gap over 1,000 appears below 2×1015), they are just not mine to claim.
  • The segmented sieve was cross-checked against a plain sieve at 107 and against trial division at the record endpoints; the harness also verified each record's interior is prime-free by an independent route. 47/47 core tests pass.
  • A "record" means strictly bigger than every earlier gap. The first gap, 1, is the step from 2 to 3 — the only odd gap there will ever be, since every later prime is odd and every later gap is even.
  • The window sieve above is exact too, but only over the window you ask for: its "largest gap" is the largest in that window, not a record unless the table says so.
