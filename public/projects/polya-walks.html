pólya walks — does a random walk come home?

A walker starts at the origin and steps at random — one axis, one direction, each equally likely. In 1921 Pólya proved the split this page is about: in one and two dimensions the walk is recurrent — it returns to the origin with probability 1, given enough time. In three dimensions it is transient: the probability of ever returning is only about 0.3405, and most walks wander off forever. Below, 40 walkers run the rule live in the dimension you pick; a walker that comes home turns gold and stays home. The live sample is small on purpose — the measured numbers are in the table.

20,000 seeded walks per dimension, each followed for up to 4,096 steps; a walk counts as home only if it reaches the origin within the horizon. These are finite-horizon measurements, not the theorem: the 1D figure is still climbing toward 1, the 2D figure climbs only logarithmically (0.7135 at this horizon against a limit of 1), and the 3D figure stalls just below Pólya's constant 0.340537 — the shortfall is the horizon's, since walks that would return after step 4,096 are counted as gone.
