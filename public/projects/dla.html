Explain it like I'm 5

Imagine tiny wanderers drifting around in the dark. When a wanderer bumps into the pile in the middle, it stops and stays there forever. That is the whole game. Nobody draws the branches — but branchy, feathery shapes grow anyway, because a wanderer is much more likely to bump into a sticky-out tip than to sneak deep into a gap. Snowflakes and coral grow by a cousin of this rule: wanderers sticking where they bump in.

Why you're looking at this

I wanted to see a complicated shape come out of a rule with almost nothing in it. This one has three lines of substance: spawn on a circle, walk at random, stick when you touch the cluster. Everything else — the dendrites, the screening of the inner bays, a fractal dimension you can measure off the finished cluster — is a consequence, not an input. The dimension readout below is measured live from the cluster on screen, not copied from a textbook, and it does not match the textbook. That gap is printed, not hidden.

how fractal is it?

Box-counting, run live over the actual cluster above: cover the cluster with boxes of size 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 cells, count how many boxes hold at least one particle, and fit a line to log(count) against log(1/size). The slope is the measured dimension D. A solid disc would read 2, a line would read 1. Big DLA clusters in the literature read about 1.71.

notes

  • Honest caveat, printed on purpose: finite clusters read noisy, and small ones read low. The reference run used for this page's preview image — seed 42, 3,000 particles — measured D = 1.42 (fit R² = 0.999 over box sizes 2–32), well under the ≈1.71 quoted for large 2D DLA clusters. The fit is clean; the cluster is just young. Tips screen the bays behind them, and a few thousand particles is not enough for the asymptotic shape to settle.
  • The growth rule never mentions branches, angles, or dimension. Walkers spawn on a circle just outside the cluster, walk one grid step at a time in one of four directions, and stick the moment they land next to an occupied cell. A walker that wanders past twice the spawn radius is discarded and respawned.
  • Same seed, same cluster, every time: the random numbers come from a seeded mulberry32 generator, and the page runs the same core logic that passed the offline test suite (16 checks). Change the seed and you get a different snowflake from the same rule.
  • Growth is incremental — a small batch of particles per animation frame — so a 3,000-particle cluster takes some seconds to finish. The dimension is recomputed as it grows, so you can watch the estimate move.
